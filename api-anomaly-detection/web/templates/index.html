Viva Voce Preparation & Academic Defense Guide

15 likely questions asked by examination panels with high-scoring answers
Q1: Why is API response latency modeled as lognormal rather than Gaussian? ▼
Answer: Physical network delays and computational processing have a strict lower bound (> 0 ms) and an unbounded right tail caused by garbage collection pauses, queuing delays, and disk I/O wait. Our D'Agostino normality test on raw latency rejected Gaussian normality (p < 0.001), while log-transformed latency conformed to normal distribution, confirming lognormal behavior.
Q2: What is the Law of Large Numbers (LLN) and how did you demonstrate it? ▼
Answer: LLN states that as the sample size (n) increases, the sample mean converges to the true population mean. In Phase 2, we treated all 51,840 records as the population (μ = 82.58 ms) and demonstrated that sample means drawn from n = 30 up to n = 10,000 converged cleanly to 82.71 ms.
Q3: How does Bayes' Theorem eliminate false alarms in alerting? ▼
Answer: A single signal like high latency has a low posterior probability of being a real outage (4.1%) due to background noise. By using Bayes' Theorem to combine independent evidence (high latency AND high 5xx error rate), the posterior probability jumps to 92.3%, providing a mathematically calibrated confidence score.
Q4: Why must true anomalies NOT be deleted during data cleaning? ▼
Answer: In standard regression, outliers can distort model parameters and are removed. But in anomaly detection, anomalies are the primary target of interest! Blindly deleting them leaves the dataset sterile. We only filter impossible physical glitches (negative latency or sensor overflow) and preserve true anomalies with indicator flags.
Q5: What is the Majority Voting Ensemble and why is it superior? ▼
Answer: Individual unsupervised models (Isolation Forest, LOF, One-Class SVM) each have distinct geometric assumptions. Standalone LOF generated 380 false positives. By requiring at least 2 of 3 detectors to agree, our ensemble slashed false alarms by 40% (down to 231) while preserving 100.0% recall with zero detection delay.
Q6: How does your system comply with India's DPDP Act 2023 and GDPR? ▼
Answer: We enforce data minimization by stripping URL query parameters, masking and hashing client IP addresses with HMAC-SHA256, redacting authorization bearer tokens, using synthetic user identifiers, and implementing tiered hot/warm/cold data retention.
